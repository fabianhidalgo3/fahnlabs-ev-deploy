# Depot simulation results

Generated by `motor/cmd/depotsim` (do not edit by hand: re-run the simulator). Offline, deterministic simulation of an overnight electric-bus depot comparing **uncoordinated charging** (baseline) with the allocation strategies of the motor's engine (`equal`, `need`, `weighted` = formulas 1-9 of the report with the contingency rule), all on the same declared inputs.

## How to reproduce

```powershell
cd motor
go run ./cmd/depotsim -scenario cmd/depotsim/scenarios -out ../docs/results
```

Every input is in the scenario files listed below; the fleet of S4 and of the fleet-size sweep is drawn by a seeded generator (same seed, same fleet). Same inputs always give the same files. `go test ./cmd/depotsim` checks the mechanics (formula 9, taper, limit, determinism, Cuadro 9).

- **C8**: Check case of the report: four buses connected at 00:00, 150 kW each, 300 kW available, no auxiliary load. The first weighted interval must reproduce Cuadro 9 (A 71.4, B 78.7, C 124.6, D 25.2 kW).
- **S1**: Overnight depot. Buses arrive 21:00-23:45 with 20-45 % SoC and leave 05:00-07:00; target 90 %, minimum 80 %. Auxiliary load (lighting, workshop, compressors) 80 kW in the evening, 50 kW at night, 90 kW from 05:00 (morning preparation).
- **S2**: Same buses, chargers and auxiliary load as S1 with the site limit at 300 kW: the depot cannot charge every bus to its target overnight, so the strategy decides who reaches the minimum SoC.
- **S3**: Early-morning services: the first five buses to arrive also leave first (02:30-04:30) with low arrival SoC, the other five leave 05:30-07:00. Limit 350 kW (23 % of the installed 1500 kW). Stresses the urgency term and the contingency rule (formula 7).

## Declared assumptions

- Time step: the physics advance in 1-minute steps; setpoints are recomputed every Δt (10 min by default) and also when a bus connects or leaves (as the motor does); `periodic_only: true` disables the event recompute.
- Setpoints are held between control instants: power a bus cannot draw because its acceptance tapers or it reaches its target mid-interval is not redistributed until the next instant (this is why strategies deliver slightly different energy under the same limit). A bus already at its target is not passed to the engine.
- SoC update: formula (9), SoC' = SoC + 100·η·P·Δt/capacity, applied every minute with the power actually drawn.
- Power drawn by a bus = min(setpoint, charger rating, battery acceptance at the current SoC, energy left to the target). The baseline has no setpoint: it draws min(charger, battery acceptance) from connection until it reaches the target.
- Battery acceptance (taper): constant maximum power up to the transition SoC (80 %), then linear down to 20 % of the maximum at 100 % SoC. Placeholder until datasheet curves are available.
- Charging efficiency η = 0.95 (grid → battery), constant. Energy figures are grid side unless stated.
- A bus stops charging at its target SoC and stays plugged in until its departure (the charger is not released unless `release_when_full` is set). Buses are assigned to free chargers first come, first served.
- Available power for the engine = site limit − safety margin (0 kW here) − highest auxiliary load inside the interval. Auxiliary load is a declared step profile, known in advance.
- Each session's cap passed to the engine = min(charger rating, battery acceptance now, energy to the target / Δt) for every coordinated strategy (the weighted strategy applies the same E/Δt bound internally, formula 8). Because of this bound, coordinated strategies differ slightly from the baseline even when the limit does not bind (the last part of each charge is spread over more intervals): see the 1500 kW row of the limit sweep.
- Engine inputs: minimum power 0 W (DC chargers can modulate to zero), Pmax for the urgency = min(charger, bus max), hours left = time to the scheduled departure, importance 1-5, weights from the scenario (report: 0.40/0.20/0.20/0.20).
- Peak demand = highest 1-minute site power (EV + auxiliary). The demand-charge estimate uses the highest 15-minute average on clock blocks (:00, :15, :30, :45).
- Charger utilization = energy delivered / (chargers × rating × hours from the first arrival to the last departure).
- Mean connection time counts from connection to departure; mean charging time counts minutes with power > 0.
- Setpoint changes = setpoints that changed by at least 100 W (the engine's granularity) at a control instant. The baseline sends none.
- Contingency activations are counted when formula (7) forecasts a departure SoC below the minimum for a bus not yet at its target.
- Coordinated strategies use all the power available up to each bus's cap (they do not shave below the limit), so their peak is essentially the site limit whenever the baseline would exceed it: the peak reduction is ≈ 1 − limit / uncoordinated peak, and the question the simulation answers is whether the departures are still met under that limit.
- Tariffs (demand charge, connection upgrade cost) are placeholders, not real prices.

## Files

- `summary.csv`: every indicator per scenario and strategy (also avoided peak and cost).
- `buses.csv`: arrival and departure SoC, energy and contingency intervals per bus.
- `sensitivity.csv`: every sweep variant and strategy, same columns as `summary.csv`.
- `timeseries/<scenario>_<strategy>.csv`: one row per control interval: auxiliary load, power available to the engine, assigned power, mean and maximum site power, limit, mean power and end SoC of each bus (for figures).
